# 10 — Typography and semantic styles

Read when defining or repairing a type system. Preserve a supplied template's
rules unless changing them is in scope.

## Compose a readable system

Choose fonts for language coverage, availability, text length, and character.
One well-used family can suffice; a contrasting display/body pair can give a
report character. Avoid arbitrary font bans and novelty pairings. Do not assume
a font exists on the recipient's system or redistribute it without a license.

For an unconstrained business document, a useful starting range is 10.5–12 pt
body text, roughly 1.1–1.3 multiple line spacing, and 6–9 pt paragraph spacing.
These are adjustable starting points, not compliance standards. Judge actual
x-height, line length, script, density, and rendering. Short memos, manuscripts,
large-print documents, and institutional templates need different settings.
Do not reduce an entire document to tiny type to meet an arbitrary page count.

Build hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, position, and restrained color.
Give headings more space above than below so they attach to following content.
Keep dense tables and notes readable. Use all caps and letter spacing for short
labels only where they help; sustained reading needs normal word shapes.
Check long titles rather than optimizing for the sample's shortest heading.

## Put repetition in styles

Map roles to native styles: body, title/subtitle, heading levels, list, caption,
quote, table text, and notes as needed. Keep heading levels logical. Use actual
outline roles for navigation and TOCs, not bold body paragraphs masquerading
as headings. Preserve custom role styles when the template already works.

Direct formatting overrides styles. Before clearing it, inspect intentional
emphasis, citations, code, symbols, and language spans. Applying a style does
not prove the displayed result changed; inherited and direct properties can
win. Inspect effective formatting or rendering where available.

Use paragraph spacing rather than repeated empty paragraphs; real tabs and
tab stops for a suitable short alignment task rather than repeated spaces.
Use genuine lists with stable indentation rather than typed bullet glyphs.
Choose paragraph spacing or first-line indentation coherently for the genre.

## Language and glyphs

Set proofing/language and appropriate script fonts when supported. Preserve
accents, symbols, mathematical notation, and nonbreaking relationships such
as a number with its unit. Check missing glyphs, fallback fonts, CJK punctuation,
and bidirectional reading order when applicable. Use native editable equations
where supported; label any necessary image fallback. Exact line heights can
clip tall glyphs, equations, and mixed scripts; verify them in rendering.
