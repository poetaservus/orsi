# 90 — Document patterns and positive treatments

Read when deciding a genre's structure. Use only the pattern relevant to the
brief; these are starting ideas, not required templates or section inventories.

| Genre | Reading task and structure | Possible visual treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision brief | Decision/request, evidence and alternatives, consequence, next action | Compact opening, aligned comparison, one clear emphasis |
| Proposal | Specific need, proposed scope, deliverables, schedule/cost, terms | Distinctive title block, strong scope table, restrained section rhythm |
| Technical report | Question, method, results, limitations, supporting detail | Precise heading system, labeled figures, legible tables, quiet body pages |
| Editorial/illustrated report | A coherent argument or story with purposeful exhibits | Expressive opening, considered image crops, varied but stable page rhythm |
| Procedure/manual | Goal/prerequisites, real ordered steps, outcomes, exceptions | Scan-friendly steps, meaningful warning treatment, adjacent illustrations |
| Letter/memo | Recipient/context, main message, necessary detail, action/sign-off | Careful type and alignment; usually no cover or contents page |
| Manuscript | The specified submission structure and citation conventions | Follow supplied publisher/institution requirements rather than a luxury theme |
| Contract/policy | Referable clauses, definitions, obligations, exceptions | Stable native numbering, clear indentation, restrained emphasis |

## Pick the right emphasis

A brief may center a comparison; a field guide may center diagrams; a report may
give an unusually important result a large clear exhibit. Surrounding pages can
be quieter. Repetition should help navigation, not give every paragraph the
same card or every page a mandatory flourish.

Use warnings when a step actually needs one, not as decorative callouts. Use
pull quotes only for real quoted language and a useful reading function. Caption
figures that need identification or explanation; do not add meaningless captions
to decorative art. Real chapter numbers and clause references are useful.

Respect the boundary between typesetting and domain authorship. A formatting
skill does not validate legal obligations, scientific conclusions, or business
claims. Retain supplied caveats and required conventions. Report a material
source contradiction within scope; do not invent a substantive answer to make
the page feel complete.

For an existing genre/template, preserve what already works. For a creative
new brief, make its subject tangible through type, composition, color, and
relevant media, with [20](20-composition-and-taste.md) guiding taste and
[80](80-review-and-evidence.md) guiding verification.
