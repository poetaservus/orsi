# 20 — Composition, anti-slop, and anti-premium-slop

Read for new visual design, redesign, polish, or design critique. Make the
subject and reading task visible in the design. Do not equate restraint with
plainness or expressiveness with decoration.

## Choose an authored direction

Translate the brief into type, alignment, page rhythm, color roles, and image
treatment. Possible directions include an ink-and-rule technical report, a
bright illustrated guide, a compact civic briefing, a warm editorial essay,
or a precise commercial proposal. These are options, not fixed themes.

Use a memorable title treatment, a clear comparison spread, purposeful art,
or a distinctive table language where the content supports it. Keep recurring
styles and alignment stable while varying the composition for real section
roles. A two-page proposal and a 40-page manual should not have the same rhythm.
Respect the user's visual references and requested intensity.

Positive choices include an asymmetric opening with a short factual deck;
a strong serif title over quiet sans-serif metadata; a single accent rule
linking headings, captions, and tables; an illustrated opener followed by
compact reading pages; or expressive color on a genuine section transition.
Choose available fonts and supported native structures. None is mandatory.

## Reject generic slop

Watch for default blue headings without a considered hierarchy, emoji section
icons, excessive bold, every thought becoming a bullet, one generic table per
section, repeated summaries, boilerplate introductions, and invented imagery
or statistics used to fill a weak outline. Newly authored headings should name
the actual topic or finding. Preserve supplied wording when editing is out of
scope. Fix structure and content relationships before adding ornament.

## Reject premium slop

More expensive-looking defaults can be equally interchangeable:

- A full-page navy/black cover with gold rules on every short memo.
- Oversized serif titles, tiny tracked uppercase labels, and acres of empty
  space that make the reader turn pages for the useful content.
- Decorative `01 / 02 / 03` counters that imply neither a sequence nor a useful
  reference system. Real chapters, procedures, and legal clauses may be numbered.
- A pull quote, tinted card, or dramatic divider on every page regardless of
  meaning; repeating the same sentence as a quote solely to occupy space.
- Invented confidential stamps, authority seals, logos, credentials, or claims.
- Tiny gray captions, thin gold text, giant watermarks, and background artwork
  that reduce print contrast or crowd the reading path.
- Treating Word pages as a stack of slide posters or website hero sections.

These elements are not globally banned. A requested luxury prospectus can use
dark covers and gold. A book may need generous margins and chapter openers.
Require a purpose, coherent execution, and readable body pages. Do not replace
one aesthetic stereotype with a universal monochrome memo.

## Review the composition

At thumbnail scale, inspect hierarchy and page-to-page rhythm. At normal size,
inspect text measure, table density, alignment, captions, and meaningful emphasis.
If the treatment could fit unrelated documents unchanged, make the actual
subject and reader task more specific in the composition. If decoration pushes
the answer later or creates thin pages, fix that. If a requested creative
redesign remains visually unchanged except for a heading color, do more.
