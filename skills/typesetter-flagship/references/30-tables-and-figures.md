# 30 — Tables, figures, and exact detail

Read for numerical exhibits, comparisons, illustrations, captions, or table repair.

## Tables must earn their structure

Use tables for relationships the reader compares across rows/columns, not to
box every paragraph. Keep explanatory prose in the reading flow. Identify
headers, units, dates, and comparison basis; preserve exact identifiers and
leading zeros. Align comparable numbers and use consistent precision suited
to meaning. Distinguish zero, missing, unavailable, and not applicable.

Choose column widths from actual content and available page width. A minimal
rule system, subtle banding, or a purposeful accent can all work. Avoid dense
grids, saturated fills everywhere, and needless merged cells. Do not shrink
long descriptions into unreadable narrow columns. Wrap labels intentionally;
use a landscape section, reorganized table, or appendix only when appropriate
and supported. Verify any split preserves row relationships and repeated keys.

Use native editable cells. For tables crossing pages, repeat real header rows
where supported. Keep short rows together; allow a genuinely tall row to split
when preventing splits would create overflow. Inspect actual page boundaries,
including wrapped cell text, footnotes, and the final row. Retain the required
paragraph after a table without letting it create an unintended blank page.

## Figures need a job

Use imagery, charts, and diagrams to explain or show something specific. Do
not invent data-backed graphics from an aesthetic prompt. Preserve source
labels, units, provenance, and meaningful uncertainty. Prefer native charts
or editable diagrams when the requested workflow supports them; disclose a
static image's limitation where future editing matters.

Size images for their final placement, maintain aspect ratio, and avoid
upscaling low-resolution detail. Inline placement usually reflows more reliably
than floating anchors. Inspect wrapping and reading order for any floated
figure. Do not turn titles, tables, or body text into images for visual control.

Place captions consistently near their exhibits and keep the label/caption
attached to the intended object. Use native caption numbering and cross-reference
fields when available; update and verify them before claiming they work.
Keep essential explanations in text, including alt text where supported. A
caption containing units or a source must remain legible when printed.

For a numerical edit, verify the changed values against the source and check
related totals/mentions within scope. Formatting alone does not establish
analytical correctness. Flag contradictions rather than silently guessing.
