# 20 — Creative composition and dashboard taste

Read for new design, redesign, polish, or critique. Build an intentional visual
identity that makes comparison enjoyable; anti-slop should not force conservatism.

## Choose an authored direction

Translate audience, brand, data, and supplied references into a specific idea:
vivid operational board, calm editorial review, compact technical console, or
commercial cockpit. Avoid applying the same navy strip and plain tables to every
brief. For an unconstrained new build, moderate design variance can mean a
recognizable KPI treatment, purposeful color/shape language, and composed views.
More creativity authorizes routine visual choices within the requested scope.

Give the primary outcome/comparison a focal treatment; keep supporting modules
in a quieter family. Comparable KPI cards can share geometry. Emphasis follows
importance, rather than random card styles or a ban on aligned rows.

## Compose before placing objects

Plan for the intended Excel window at a readable zoom. Map header/context, KPIs,
controls, main views, and reachable detail before writing or drawing. Estimate
module footprints, label lengths, row/column geometry, and shared anchors/gutters.
Allow for legends, long values, and the filter region. Resolve overlaps and
oversized gaps in this plan instead of accumulating objects and rearranging later.
An inferred layout does not need separate approval for an ordinary build.

Keep the main comparison visible with the KPIs, directly beside/below them where
practical. Use available width before extending downward. A single-screen summary
is useful when legible; supporting detail may scroll. Do not shrink the whole
workbook to force every table into one view.

Move helper summaries and long methodology notes to supporting areas/sheets when
supported and in scope, preserving their links. Keep a compact period/unit line
near the results. Do not delete sources to clear the canvas.

Possible arrangements, chosen for the decision:
- Comparison cockpit: KPIs, broad trend, ranked bars, compact selectors/detail.
- Exception board: priority table and status/aging view; cards supply context.
- Variance review: focal outcome, actual/plan or contribution view, explanation.
- Cohort/portfolio review: matrix, scatter, or concentration view with totals.

These are options, not templates or chart quotas. Align module edges, plot areas,
and gutters; use whitespace to separate meaning and permit expected expansion.

## KPI-card families

| Family | Treatment | Useful when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accent rule | Light surface, fine outline, small label, large value, bottom/side accent | Compact comparisons |
| Tinted icon | Soft solid/two-tone fill or subtle gradient, modest corners, clear icon zone | Expressive coordinated KPIs |
| Focal | Stronger background/accent, high-contrast value, quiet context | One outcome deserves emphasis |
| Trend | Value, period/delta, real sparkline with stable alignment | Comparable history exists |
| Target | Actual, progress/bullet comparison, target/variance | Compatible target exists |

Link shape/text-box values to live cells when supported; otherwise use styled
live cells. Static pasted numbers do not make an updateable KPI. Colored accents
may identify metric families. Status colors and directional indicators follow
[10 — Data and metrics](10-data-and-metrics.md); comparison absence must not look
like a successful result. Use a non-directional identity icon or omit the arrow
when history is unavailable. Design sample numbers are placeholders, not facts.

## Native shapes, icons, and color

Shapes can provide containment, badges, accents, and focus. Use consistent icon
weight/size with labels: currency, order, unit, time, or alerts where meaningful.
Avoid unrelated emoji styles and icons on every cell. Native conditional icon
sets can express defined statuses. Pair semantic color with words or another cue.

Subtle gradients, softened corners, and restrained shadows are welcome when
supported and readable. Keep backgrounds quiet behind labels; verify geometry
after resizing/saving. Cells with deliberate fills, rules, spacing, and typography
remain a valid creative route when drawings are unavailable.

Define background, text, accent, comparison, category, and status color roles.
Use saturation for selected accents/focal modules; preserve category meaning
after filtering. Do not distribute red/orange/green merely to add visual variety.

## Compose charts, slicers, and tables together

Choose encodings for distinct questions: trends, ranked bars, period columns,
actual/target, composition, waterfall, scatter, or heatmap when suitable/supported.
Style plot areas, labels, backgrounds, and cards as one system. One clear chart
can outperform several repetitive ones. Read [30 — Charts and comparisons](30-charts-and-comparisons.md).

Slicers need readable captions, aligned edges, coherent selected states, and
compact placement near affected content. Tables/PivotTables can supply filtering
and aggregation; manually formatted presentation ranges are also valid. Use
header bands, subtle row grouping, aligned numbers, total emphasis, status markers,
or data bars purposefully. Keep formulas live and inspect formatting after refresh.

## Typography and finish

Create hierarchy for title, label, value, axis/table text, and notes using available
fonts. Keep values numeric and formats appropriate. At normal size, inspect the
initial window and whole sheet: focal point, balanced color, useful density,
consistent families, visible comparisons, no overlap or clipping. Correct layout
before adding more objects. Reject fake deltas/controls, arbitrary counters, 3D
distortion, and empty regions that impede comparison. Preserve expressive choices
that serve the brief; a generic worksheet does not fulfill a designed dashboard.
